Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Reston

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. While the space overhead stays damp, the stain returns, and within 24 to 48 hours mold can begin on the drywall paper. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. Sagging changes the picture. Keep clear of that area and phone us.

What It Looks Like

  • Patches of spotty discoloration that look like mold on the ceiling
  • Paint that bubbles, blisters or peels away from the ceiling surface
  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
  • Stale, mildew like odor you cannot trace to anything you can see
  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
What We Do

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Trace the Leak

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. Until the water stops coming, fixing the ceiling would not last.

2

Secure the Room

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Open and Dry

Wet insulation is removed along with any drywall that cannot stay. The joists and nearby ceiling are dried with air movers plus a dehumidifier, and antimicrobial goes on wherever the water was dirty.

4

Rebuild and Paint

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Spreads Above

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. Because the panel soaks from its top side, nothing may look wrong from underneath.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. The paper layer becomes a place mold is able to grow, and heavy wet insulation bears down from above.

Day 3 to 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

Past one week

Sections May Fall

Parts of the ceiling can come down along with the wet insulation resting on them. By now mold has settled into the space above, and the area needing repair has grown considerably.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

Because moisture runs along framing, plumbing and ductwork, the drip may be feet away from the entry point. We check beyond the visible stain so soaked sections down the line are not overlooked.

Damage on the Floor Above

Whatever is over the ceiling, a subfloor or attic decking, has typically been soaked as well. Our inspection includes the space overhead, since moisture left there keeps feeding the problem.

Growth on the Hidden Side

The top side of the drywall and the insulation resting on it stay wet long after the surface feels dry, which is where mold tends to grow unseen.

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Asbestos was used in certain older plasters and popcorn finishes. Testing comes first in such a home, ahead of any cutting, scraping or demolition of the damaged section.
