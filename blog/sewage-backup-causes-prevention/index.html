Why Sewage Backups Happen in Reston, VA

Home / Blog / Why Sewage Backups Happen in Reston, VA

Few household emergencies are as unpleasant as sewage backing up into the home. The result is extensive damage, an immediate threat to health, and a professional cleanup bill that can reach thousands of dollars whenever raw sewage enters the rooms you live in. Your risk drops significantly when you know what causes backups and take steps to prevent them. This guide from Reston Restoration Co covers the common causes of sewage backups in Reston, VA and practical steps you can take to protect your property.

Common Causes of Sewage Backups

When something keeps wastewater from draining to the city sewer or your septic tank as designed, it comes back into the building. Usual culprits are tree roots growing into sewer pipes; lines clogged by a buildup of hair, grease, and debris; pipes that are old or have collapsed; combined sewer overflow events; an overloaded municipal system in heavy rain; and toilets used to flush the wrong things, such as feminine products, wipes, and too much paper.

Tree Roots in the Sewer Line

Roots from nearby trees are a leading reason sewer lines in Reston get blocked. A sewer pipe is a steady supply of moisture, which is exactly what roots go looking for. Entry happens at connection points, cracks, and joints, and over time the growth packs the pipe, creating the blockages behind many backups. Older sewer lines made of clay tile are especially easy for roots to invade. Schedule a sewer camera inspection every 2 to 3 years so root trouble is found ahead of any backup.

Prevention Strategies

To guard a Reston house against backups, start by having a backwater valve fitted to your sewer connection so the city system cannot flow back in. Keep cooking oil and grease out of every drain. Only flush toilet paper, never wipes, feminine products, or other items. Routine inspection and cleaning of your sewer line should be on the calendar. Deal with a slow drain quickly, before it turns into a full blockage. Know the location of your sewer cleanout for emergency access. Think about adding a sewage backup endorsement to your homeowner's policy, or buying sewer line insurance.

Sewage Came Up? Do This First

When sewage backs up, leave the cleanup alone. Serious illness can come from the dangerous pathogens in sewage, among them parasites, bacteria, and viruses.
