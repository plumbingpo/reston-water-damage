Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Wherever flooding is likely, get appliances and electrical equipment up above floor level
  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors
  • Check that grading on all sides of the house carries water away from the foundation
  • Debris in a gutter or storm drain blocks proper flow, so clean them out regularly
  • Look into a flood insurance policy, including when your home sits outside a mapped flood zone
  • Use waterproof bins and raised shelving for anything valuable kept at ground level or below

What a Professional Brings to Flood Cleanup

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. The water brings sewage, chemicals, bacteria and more with it, and all of that is a serious hazard to health. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Beyond the health concerns, a poor flood cleanup often leaves hidden moisture that turns into severe mold growth within days. Our technicians rely on psychrometric calculations, meter readings and thermal imaging to verify that each affected spot, from the framing to the subfloor to the inside of the walls, meets accepted drying standards. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.

Signs It Is Time for Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or drywall that feels soft and wet
  • Water coming in through foundation cracks, window wells or door thresholds
  • Stains along baseboards and lower walls that mark the flood level
  • Soaked landscaping that leaves water pooling against your foundation
  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • A sump pump that failed during heavy rain and let the basement flood
  • Living areas flooded by contaminated water from a backed-up drain or sewer
