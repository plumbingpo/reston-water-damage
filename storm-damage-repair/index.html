Why Storm Damage Repair Is Not a DIY Job

Because one kind of storm damage feeds another, the work needs restoration professionals with real experience. A roof leak that looks minor from inside may have done extensive hidden damage to insulation, roof decking and wall cavities, and that leads to mold growth and structural deterioration if it is not found and dried. With moisture meters and thermal cameras, trained technicians follow the route water took and find all the areas it reached.

Temporary protection such as tarps and board-up needs correct technique or it will not hold. An improperly installed tarp can create new problems by channeling water or failing under wind load. Experienced in storm mitigation, our team picks appropriate methods and materials to keep the building protected until permanent repair work can be scheduled.

Maintenance Tips for Storm Damage Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Branches break in high wind, so keep nearby trees pruned away from the roof and siding
  • Clogged gutters overflow in a downpour, so clear them and the downspouts often
  • Make sure the ground around the house slopes away and downspouts discharge well clear of it
  • Schedule a roof inspection every year so weak spots are repaired before storm season
  • Photograph your property once a year so its condition is on record for insurance purposes
  • If storm damage breaks a pipe you will need the main shutoff, so find it and practice now

How to Tell You Need Storm Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Standing water in attic spaces, especially by skylights and chimneys
  • A basement or ground floor that floods when the drains cannot keep up
  • Attic or wall insulation that is damp or matted down following a storm
  • From ground level, the roof shows shingles torn off, broken or raised
  • Rain blown in around door frames, window frames or openings in the wall
  • Bubbling paint and soft, drooping spots overhead once the rain stops
  • Metal flashing pulled away from vents, valleys or the edge of the roof
