Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Reston

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Stay Out of the WaterNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A failed check valve that lets pumped water run back into the pit.
  • Undersized equipment that falls behind when inflow is heavy.
  • Gravel, silt or debris in the pit jamming the impeller or clogging the intake.
  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Wall Framing

The wood plate at the base of each wall sits right on the slab and soaks up water first. Once the drywall is off, the plates and studs are washed, disinfected and dried.

Low Electrical Outlets

Basement outlets sit low on the wall, where rising water can reach them. Before those circuits are used again, an electrician needs to inspect whatever was under water.

Basement Floor Coverings

Carpet and pad soaked by groundwater cannot be cleaned to a safe standard and are removed. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Drywall and Wall Insulation

The wet zone inside a wall reaches well past the water mark, as drywall and insulation pull moisture up off the floor. Because the water is contaminated, the wet lower section is cut out, not dried.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Often the only coverage comes from an added endorsement for water backup and sump overflow, which has limits of its own. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Pump Out the Basement

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Clean and Sanitize

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

We run drying equipment until our meters show normal moisture in the slab and the wood, which typically takes 3 to 5 days. Make sure a functioning pump is installed again by the time drying wraps up.
