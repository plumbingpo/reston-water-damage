Wet Carpet Drying: What Reston Homeowners Should Know

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. In Reston, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Signs to Look For

  • Carpet that has come off the tack strip, leaving loose areas or ripples
  • Brown or yellow stains wicking up to the tips of the fibers as it dries
  • Musty, sour odor that builds whenever the door and windows stay shut
  • Paint blistering near the floor, or baseboard trim that has puffed up
  • A floor that squeaks or feels soft under the carpet where it got wet
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Day 3 to 7

Carpet Starts Failing

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Wet Wall Bottoms

Under the carpet, water travels until it hits the bottom of the walls. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.

Mold Under the Carpet

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.

Stains From Furniture

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

Our Process

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reset

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.
