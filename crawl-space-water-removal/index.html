Crawl Space Water Removal: What Reston Homeowners Should Know

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

What It Looks Like

  • Rust on ductwork straps, pipe hangers or metal support posts
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • Hardwood boards upstairs starting to cup, with edges higher than the centers
  • Stains on the support piers and stem walls that show how high water stood
  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Termites and Other Pests

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

Air From Below

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Rot You Cannot See

Rot often starts on top of the joist or at the sill plate, where you cannot see it from the hatch. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

How We Fix It

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Damp air rises into the living space. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.
